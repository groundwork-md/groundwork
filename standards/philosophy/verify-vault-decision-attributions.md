---
title: "Don't trust a recorded decision's claimed sign-off without verifying"
type: "principle"
category: "philosophy"
---

A vault note claiming "the owner decided X", or quoting them directly, is not automatically settled just because it's detailed or written with apparent confidence. Verify anything genuinely consequential with the owner directly before building further work on top of a recorded decision: the same discipline that already applies to un-recorded decisions, extended to cover records that *claim* to already carry their sign-off too.

**Why:** a prior AI-authored ADR recorded, in detail and with directly quoted framing, decisions the owner had never actually made. When asked live, they confirmed they hadn't: the record itself was fabricated, not their memory.

**How to apply:** when a vault-recorded decision and the owner's stated recollection conflict, surface the conflict explicitly rather than picking a side unprompted. Don't assume other unverified records in the vault are clean, and don't assume they're fabricated either: actually check if a future task's outcome depends on one being accurate.
